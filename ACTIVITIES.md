## Workshop Reports

### Basic Cybersecurity and Ethical Hacking for First-Year Students

**Date:** 15th March 2024
**Organized by:** Aegis — Cybersecurity and Ethical Hacking Club

#### Overview
On the 15th of March 2024, the Cybersecurity and Ethical Hacking Club conducted an enlightening workshop aimed at first-year students. The workshop aimed to provide foundational knowledge in cybersecurity and ethical hacking, equipping attendees with essential skills and insights into the field.

#### Sessions

**1. MITM and Basics of Networking — by Shivangi**
Shivangi led the first session, delving into the concepts of Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attacks and the fundamentals of networking. Through interactive discussions and practical examples, attendees gained a deeper understanding of how data interception occurs and the underlying principles of network communication.

**2. WiFi Password Retrieval and Python Keylogger — by Sarah**
Sarah took charge of the second session, focusing on practical techniques such as WiFi password retrieval and the implementation of a Python keylogger. Participants learned about the importance of securing their WiFi networks and the potential risks associated with unauthorized access. Additionally, Sarah demonstrated how Python programming could be leveraged to develop a keylogging tool, highlighting the importance of responsible usage and ethical considerations.

**3. Social Engineering and AI's Harmful Potential — by Suryateja**
Suryateja led the third session, shedding light on the deceptive tactics of social engineering and the detrimental effects of artificial intelligence (AI) when misused. Through case studies and real-world examples, attendees gained insights into the psychological manipulation techniques employed by cybercriminals and the ethical implications of AI technology. Suryateja emphasized the need for ethical behavior and responsible innovation in the cybersecurity landscape.

**4. Capture The Flag (CTF) Challenge — by Kishore**
The workshop concluded with an engaging Capture The Flag (CTF) challenge, allowing students to apply their newfound knowledge in a practical setting. The CTF covered various cybersecurity-related topics, providing participants with an opportunity to test their skills and problem-solving abilities in a simulated environment.

#### Conclusion
The workshop on Basic Cybersecurity and Ethical Hacking proved to be a valuable learning experience for first-year students, offering a comprehensive overview of essential concepts and practical applications in the field. Through interactive sessions and hands-on activities, attendees were equipped with the foundational knowledge and skills necessary to navigate the evolving landscape of cybersecurity responsibly.

The Cybersecurity and Ethical Hacking Club remains committed to fostering awareness and expertise in cybersecurity among students, paving the way for a more secure and resilient digital future.

### 5-Day Cybersecurity Workshop (27–31 October 2025)

**Date:** 27th – 31st October 2025
**Organized by:** Aegis — Cybersecurity and Ethical Hacking Club

#### Overview
Aegis RVITM recently hosted a cybersecurity workshop, held from 27th to 31st October 2025, led by Samarth sir and Niharika P Kumar ma'am , offering valuable, hands-on insights into today's threat landscape.

**Proactive Threat Mitigation**
Practical approaches to spotting and responding to common cyber threats.

**Best Practices for Secure Systems**
An in-depth look at password hygiene, multi-factor authentication, and secure network setup.

**Ethical Hacking Fundamentals**
Core principles that strengthen an organization's overall defense posture.

#### Conclusion
The workshop proved to be an excellent professional development opportunity, underscoring how essential continuous learning is in the ever-evolving field of digital security.
